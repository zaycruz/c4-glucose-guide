Challenge 4 · Meals, timing and movement

A walk after a meal: honest about how much it helps

From 12 weeks of glucose sensor data, it measures how meals, meal timing and movement shape each survivor's blood sugar, and turns that into advice they can follow. Every number in the advice traces to its evidence.

What it means for a care team

A short walk after a meal is low-risk, but real data does not yet show it lowers the sugar rise. The large effect is in the synthetic program only. mg/dL is the unit on a glucose meter.

What to do

Offer a short walk after a meal and an earlier dinner as habits to try, not proven fixes. Survivors with lows get one message: call the care team today, with no exercise or meal-time advice.

For each survivor

A forecast of their own dinner rise

Gradient boosting forecasts the rise after a survivor's typical dinner, each meal by a model that never saw it. Typical miss 14 mg/dL. Personal effects are shown only where the person's own data supports them; otherwise the advice says so and uses the cohort number.

Know the limits

  • Walking was self-chosen, so the walk effect is an association. It passes a negative control.
  • In real people the effects point the same way but are much smaller and not proven yet.
  • Night-shift workers get no clock-time advice.

Safety routing

Who gets a call today

  • Care team today when more than 4% of readings are below 70 mg/dL, more than 1% are below 54 (level 2 lows), the average is above 250, or more than 5% of readings are above 250. 10 of 50 survivors today.
  • Next visit for other alerts (time in range under 70%). 21 of 50 are alert or watch, but only the 10 above interrupt anyone.
  • Lows mean care team first: one card, no walking, fasting or meal-time advice, the 15-15 warning, and a medication check. Medicines are not recorded in this data, so it asks about insulin or a sulfonylurea (glipizide, glyburide, glimepiride).
  • Spanish and Kreyòl patient text is machine-translated and not yet reviewed by a native speaker; each language is marked in the API.

Fairness by group

Does it work the same for everyone?

In plain words: after correcting for 10 tests at once (Holm), only the walk effect by sex differs (larger in men, p = 0.02). The forecast miss is similar across groups. Before real use, recompute this every quarter on real patients; a group that differs gets the pooled number and care-team review instead of automatic advice. Synthetic data: this checks the engine, not real-world fairness. Miss is the forecast's typical error in mg/dL; walk and late dinner are effects in mg/dL. Endpoints

JSON over HTTPS, no key needed. Contract in CONTRACT.md.

  • GET/api/c4/→Index: the module, its data type and every route.
  • GET/api/c4/survivors→The roster: 50 survivors with status, a plain headline and up to four signal tags.
  • GET/api/c4/survivors/:id→One survivor: recommendations, each number tied to evidence, safety flags and a patient view in English, Spanish and Kreyòl.
  • GET/api/c4/survivors/:id/advice→Warm, plain-language advice. Precomputed; rewrites with untraced numbers are rejected.
  • GET/api/c4/cohort→Program-wide effects with CIs, the randomized trial, the real-data check (NHANES survey-weighted), the fairness table and a plain-language summary.
  • GET/api/c4/verification→The published verification record (commit, data hash, seeds, reproduce commands) that the end-to-end check compares with the live data.

Reproduce

Raw data stays out of the repo; only the derived JSON is committed.

github.com/zaycruz/c4-glucose-guide →
  1. git clone https://github.com/zaycruz/c4-glucose-guide && cd c4-glucose-guide

    Needs uv. Pinned Python 3.12 environment in uv.lock.

  2. D=/path/to/Challenge4_Dose_and_Timing

    The Challenge 4 download; checksums in data/SHA256SUMS, sources in data/README.md.

  3. scripts/reproduce.sh $D

    About 10 to 15 minutes on 4 vCPU. Runs the engine, the benchmark and the evidence, then checks all 52 README numbers. Proof run from a fresh clone on Cloudflare: runs/reproduce-20261017T171329Z.md (REPRODUCE.md).

  4. node scripts/e2e.mjs --strict

    Checks every endpoint, that every number in every recommendation is in its cited evidence, and that /verification matches the live data.
